Objective And Convexity 03 / 12

\[ \min_x f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \left[ c_h D_i^d \left(\frac{D_i^d - x_i^d}{D_i^d}\right)^2 + c_e D_i^c \left(\frac{D_i^c - x_i^c}{D_i^c}\right)^2 \right] \]

Normalization

Dividing shortfall by baseline demand penalizes percentage cuts, preventing large states from dominating the allocation.

Squared Loss

A 20% cut is four times harsher than a 10% cut, so the solver spreads pain instead of concentrating it.

Strict Convexity

\[ H = \text{diag}(2A_1,\dots,2A_n,2B_1,\dots,2B_n) \]

- $w_i \ge 0.20$ by construction.

- $c_h, c_e > 0$ from financial coefficients.

- $D_i > 0$ for all baseline demands.

Result: Positive definite Hessian and unique global optimum.

Penalty Coefficients 06 / 12

Health Penalty ($c_h$)

Converts mortality and morbidity risk from biomass reversion into an optimization coefficient.

1. Disease burden: 0.05 DALYs/year for biomass households.

2. Monthly burden: 0.004 DALYs/month.

3. VSL conversion: India's Value of Statistical Life around INR 1.33 million per DALY.

Survival Chance Constraint 07 / 12

Log-Normal Survival Model

Survival demand is right-skewed during crisis because panic buying and logistics shocks create fat-tail risk.

\[ P(x_i^d \ge \tilde{\theta}_i) \ge 0.95 \]
\[ \sigma_{\log}^2 = \ln(1 + \text{CV}^2) \]
\[ \mu_{\log} = \ln(\mu_i) - \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\log}^2 \]
\[ \theta_i = F^{-1}(0.95; \mu_{\log}, \sigma_{\log}) \]

R Implementation I 08 / 12

Matrix Formulation Code

# Pure Matrix Formulation for quadprog
# ---------------------------------------------------------

# Dmat and dvec (Proportional Normalization applied)
diag_elements = c( (2 * w_i * c_h) / D_id, (2 * w_i * c_e) / D_ic )
Dmat = diag(diag_elements)
Dmat = Dmat + diag(1e-8, 2 * n)
dvec = c( rep(2 * c_h, n) * w_i, rep(2 * c_e, n) * w_i )

# Constraint 1: Sum of all allocations == S_usable
A_sum = rep(1, 2 * n)
b_sum = S_usable

# Constraint 2: x_id >= theta_i (Stochastic Survival)
A_theta = rbind(diag(n), matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = n))
b_theta = theta_i

# Constraint 3: x_id <= D_id => -x_id >= -D_id
A_did = rbind(-diag(n), matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = n))
b_did = -D_id

# Constraint 4: x_ic >= 0.25 * D_ic
A_xic_floor = rbind(matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = n), diag(n))
b_xic_floor = 0.25 * D_ic

# Constraint 5: x_id >= 0.40 * D_id
A_xid_floor = rbind(diag(n), matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = n))
b_xid_floor = 0.40 * D_id

# Constraint 6: x_ic <= 0.70 * D_ic
A_dic = rbind(matrix(0, nrow = n, ncol = n), -diag(n))
b_dic = -0.70 * D_ic

Amat = cbind(A_sum, A_theta, A_did, A_xic_floor, A_xid_floor, A_dic)
bvec = c(b_sum, b_theta, b_did, b_xic_floor, b_xid_floor, b_dic)

What This Builds

Quadratic Penalty Matrix

Dmat stores the normalized quadratic penalties for domestic and commercial shortages. The small ridge term keeps the matrix numerically positive definite.

Linear Term

dvec contains the linear part of the expanded squared-loss objective, weighted by vulnerability.

Constraint Matrix

Amat stacks the total supply rule, survival floors, no-overallocation rule, and commercial cap into quadprog form.

Constraint Vector

bvec stores the corresponding right-hand-side values for every policy and physical constraint.

Results Dashboard 11 / 12

Analytical Findings

Equity Floors

Delhi and Tamil Nadu hit the 40% domestic floor exactly, which means the political equity constraint is binding. Without this rule, the optimizer would push more shortage onto cleaner-fuel states to protect high-biomass regions.

DALY Minimization

Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, and Rajasthan retain the highest domestic satisfaction levels. The model identifies that LPG cuts in these states create larger expected health damage because fallback cooking options are more hazardous.

Squeezed Middle

Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Karnataka sit between the protected and floor-bound groups. They are not fragile enough to receive maximum shielding, but not resilient enough to fall to the minimum floor, so they absorb the model's balancing pressure.

Commercial Caps

For several vulnerable states, commercial allocation reaches the 70% cap. This shows the optimizer would otherwise divert more LPG from commerce to households, but the cap preserves economic continuity and reduces long-run business scarring.

The model trades commercial output for health protection, but policy floors keep the solution politically feasible.

Limitations 12 / 12

Technical Limitations

Economic Inelasticity

The model treats commercial LPG as a near-perfect bottleneck for food and accommodation activity. In reality, some firms may switch to electricity, PNG, diesel, or temporary menu reductions, so $c_e$ may overstate short-run economic loss.

Static Month

The optimization is solved as a single-month allocation. It does not capture inventory carryover, repeated household coping behavior, firm bankruptcies, or compounding welfare losses in month two or three of a prolonged disruption.

State Aggregation

Each state is modeled as one allocation unit. This hides district-level shortages, last-mile delivery failures, leakage, local political prioritization, and the rural-urban split inside large states.
